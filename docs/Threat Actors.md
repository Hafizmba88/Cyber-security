# Threat actor types

## Advanced persistent threats (APTs)

Advanced persistent threats (APTs) have significant expertise accessing an organization's network without authorization. APTs research targets (e.g., large corporations or government entities) and can remain undetected for an extended period.

Intentions and motivations:

- Damaging critical infrastructure (e.g., power grid, natural resources)
- Gaining access to intellectual property (trade secrets, patents)
- Espionage and long-term data exfiltration

## Insider threats

Insider threats abuse authorized access to obtain or expose data that may harm an organization.

Intentions and motivations:

- Sabotage
- Corruption
- Espionage
- Unauthorized data access or leaks

Insiders can be negligent (accidental) or malicious (intentional).

## Hacktivists

Hacktivists are threat actors driven by a political or social agenda. They abuse digital technology to accomplish their goals.

Common goals:

- Demonstrations
- Propaganda
- Social change campaigns
- Notoriety / fame

Typical tactics include website defacement, data leaks, and DDoS attacks timed for political effect.

## Hacker types

A hacker is any person who uses computers to gain access to systems, networks, or data. Hackers vary in skill and motivation. Three main categories by authorization/intent:

- Authorized hackers (ethical hackers)
  - Also called white-hat or ethical hackers.
  - Conduct legal risk assessments, follow codes of conduct, and help secure organizations.

- Semi-authorized hackers (researchers)
  - Search for vulnerabilities and typically disclose findings without exploiting them for gain.

- Unauthorized hackers (unethical hackers)
  - Malicious actors who break laws to steal, sell, or expose confidential data for financial or other gain.

Other hacker types and motivations:

- New and unskilled actors ("script kiddies")
  - Motivations: learn skills, revenge, notoriety; rely on existing malware or scripts.

- Contracted hackers / mercenaries
  - Perform tasks for pay; may operate ethically or unethically depending on the contract.

- Vigilantes
  - Self-appointed actors who attempt to stop perceived criminals, sometimes acting outside the law.

> Note: Many individuals or groups may fall into more than one category depending on context and actions.

## Key takeaways

- "Threat actor" describes motive and malicious intent; "hacker" describes technical skill and methods.
- APTs = capability + persistence; Insiders = authorized access abused; Hacktivists = political/social motives.
- Understanding motives and capabilities helps prioritize defenses: detection & monitoring for APTs; least privilege, DLP and behavior analytics for insiders; robust public-facing protections and communications for hacktivists.

## Defensive implications (brief)

- APTs: network segmentation, threat hunting, long-term log retention, EDR/XDR.
- Insiders: least privilege, access reviews, DLP, separation of duties, behavioral analytics.
- Hacktivists: WAF, DDoS protection, incident response and communication plans.
